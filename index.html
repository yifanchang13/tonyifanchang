Prior Work

Directional Trust and Venture Capital Investments: Evidence from China

Undergraduate Thesis

Bottazzi et al. (2016) show that generalized trust between nations facilitates cross-border venture capital investment but lowers investors' participation threshold, thereby inducing investment in marginal companies with lower success probabilities and impairing performance. However, the symmetric, nation-level trust measure obscures potentially asymmetric effects of directional trust between VC investors and investees. In this paper, we exploit the asymmetric structure of inter-provincial trust in China to examine how directional trust between VC investors and investees shapes VC deal structure and performance. Using a comprehensive sample of cross-province VC deals in China from 2000 to 2022 and a survey-based measure of inter-provincial trust from the Chinese Entrepreneurs Survey System (CESS), we find that higher investee-to-investor trust is associated with larger investment amounts, earlier investment stages, and a lower syndication likelihood, consistent with entrepreneurs being more willing to cede bargaining power and accept concentrated ownership when they trust their investors. Conversely, higher investor-to-investee trust predicts higher pre-money valuations and worse investment performance, consistent with investors' trust-induced generosity in deal pricing and laxity in ex-ante screening. Our findings are robust to instrumenting directional trust with genetic and surname distances across provinces.
